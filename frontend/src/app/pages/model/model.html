How the numbers are made

A zeolite column is fed with a single refrigerant gas at 100 °C and 0.8 bar. The bed adsorbs it until it is saturated, then the refrigerant starts to appear at the outlet. The moment the outlet reaches a chosen fraction of the feed concentration (c/c₀ = 5 % by default) is the breakthrough time. Everything below leads to that number.

The calculation chain

Formulas as used in corrected mode. Where thesis mode does something else, it says so; the audit below explains why it was changed.

  1. Equilibrium

    The adsorbed amount q at 0.8 bar comes from published isotherms (thesis Tab. 2). With the bulk density of the bed it becomes the adsorbed mass per bed volume, and over the gas concentration it gives the Henry constant Ka. Rosen's model needs it per volume of the grains, plus the gas held in the grains' macropores.

    c* = pM / RT    av = q ρn M    Ka = av / c*    K = Ka / (1 − ε) + εM

    Thesis: K = Ka.

  2. Bed structure

    From the measurements in chapter 4 of the thesis: grain volume Vk, grain mass mk, bulk density ρn of a poured bed and the zeolite pore volume vp. The space between the grains follows from the bulk and the grain (envelope) density.

    ε = 1 − ρn / (mk / Vk)    z = ε / (1 − ε)    w = ṁ / (ρ A ε)

    Thesis: ε = (ρp − ρn) / ρp with ρp = mk / (Vk − vpmk).

  3. Transport into the grain

    The grains are beads of small zeolite crystals held together by a binder. Gas reaches the crystals through the binder's macropores (around half a micrometre) and adsorbs along the way, which is what normally limits the uptake of such beads (Ruthven 1984; Hu et al. 2014). In the macropores molecular and Knudsen diffusion act in series [11] and the path is longer by the tortuosity τ. Diffusion inside the crystals is taken as fast.

    D = 1.32 μ / ρ    DK = dM/3 · √(8RT / πM)    1 / Dp = τ (1 / D + 1 / DK)

    K D′ = εM Dp

    The first is the Chapman–Enskog result for self-diffusion [9], ρD/μ = 6A*/5 ≈ 1.32, which only needs the measured viscosity. The last line maps the macropore model onto Rosen's grain diffusion: both give the same flux into a grain for a linear isotherm.

    Thesis: kinetic theory of hard spheres for D, Knudsen in the zeolite window (dp = 4–9 Å) for DK, 1 / D′ = 1 / D + 1 / DK, no tortuosity.

  4. Flow and film

    Reynolds and Schmidt numbers go into a Sherwood correlation for packed beds of spheres, which gives the film mass transfer coefficient kc,g.

    Sh = 2 + 1.1 Sc1/3 Rep0.6    Rep = wk dp ρ / μ    kc,g = Sh D / dp

    Thesis: Sh = 0.395 Re0.54 Sc0.33 [3] with the equivalent channel diameter dz as the length.

  5. Rosen solution

    For a linear isotherm, diffusion into spherical grains and an external film, Rosen gave a closed form for the outlet curve [2], [3]. Two dimensionless groups decide its shape.

    λ′ = 3D′KH / (z w R²)    ψ = D′K / (R kc,g)

    c/c₀ = ½ [1 + erf(E)]    E = (3τ′ / 2λ′ − 1) / (2 √((1 + 5ψ) / 5λ′))    τ′ = 2D′ / R² (t − H/w)

    At c/c₀ = 0.5 this is exactly the mass balance: bed capacity over feed, plus the gas residence time.

  6. Numerical check

    Rosen ignores axial dispersion and only gives the outlet. The numerical model solves the column equations directly with a linear driving force for the grains and Edwards–Richardson dispersion, on 200 finite volumes. With kLDF = 15D′ / (R²(1 + 5ψ)) (Glueckauf 1955) it has the same front width as Rosen when dispersion is switched off, and the test suite checks exactly that. Why such a simple driving force works so well is discussed by Sircar & Hufton (2000).

The chain itself (isotherm point → Henry constant → diffusion → film → Rosen) is the standard way to size a bed for a first estimate, and all inputs come from the same measurements as in the thesis. The simulator shows every intermediate value in both modes next to each other.

What the model leaves out

  • Only one refrigerant at a time. Mixtures need competitive isotherms (IAST or multicomponent Langmuir); the separation page overlays single curves as a first estimate. Yancey et al. (2022) review refrigerant isotherms on zeolites and other sorbents.
  • A linear isotherm through one point at 0.8 bar. Near saturation the real isotherm is curved and the front self-sharpens.
  • Constant temperature. Heat of adsorption warms the bed and shortens the breakthrough in practice; at high concentrations the uptake of zeolite beads can become limited by heat removal rather than diffusion (Hu et al. 2014).
  • An undiluted feed. Rosen and the LDF model are trace-component theories with a constant gas velocity. With pure refrigerant the gas slows down as it is adsorbed, and the flux into the macropores is Knudsen plus viscous flow rather than diffusion through a carrier gas. Treat the shape of the front as indicative; the stoichiometric time does not depend on it.
  • Diffusion inside the zeolite crystals is taken as fast. For R22 and R125 in 4A the molecules are about as wide as the window, and crystal diffusion may well be the slowest step; only uptake measurements (ZLC, volumetric) would settle it.
  • Macropore data measured for commercial 13X beads are assumed for 4A and 5A as well.
  • The voidage was measured in a 53 ml vessel with 4–7.5 mm grains. The wall makes it higher than in a 0.5 m column, where randomly poured spheres give 0.36–0.42.
  • For 13X the Rosen length parameter drops to about 15 in corrected mode, where the asymptotic (erf) form is least accurate; compare with the numerical curve.
  • Gas properties at 100 °C only, viscosities from CoolProp [12].
