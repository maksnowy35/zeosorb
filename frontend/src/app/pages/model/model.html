How the numbers are made

A zeolite column is fed with a single refrigerant gas at 100 °C and 0.8 bar. The bed adsorbs it until it is saturated, then the refrigerant starts to appear at the outlet. The moment the outlet reaches a chosen fraction of the feed concentration (c/c₀ = 5 % by default) is the breakthrough time. Everything below leads to that number.

The calculation chain

  1. Equilibrium

    The adsorbed amount q at 0.8 bar comes from published isotherms. With the bulk density of the bed it becomes the adsorbed mass per bed volume, and over the gas concentration it gives the Henry constant.

    c* = pM / RT    av = q ρbulk M    Ka = av / c*

  2. Diffusion

    Molecular diffusion from the kinetic theory of gases, Knudsen diffusion in the zeolite pores, combined in series (and divided by the pore tortuosity if you set one).

    1 / D′ = 1 / D + 1 / DK    DK = dp/3 · √(8RT / πM)

  3. Flow and film

    The mass flow gives the gas velocity between the grains. Reynolds and Schmidt numbers go into a Sherwood correlation, which gives the film mass transfer coefficient kc,g.

    w = ṁ / (ρ A ε)    kc,g = Sh D / L

  4. Rosen solution

    For a linear isotherm, diffusion into spherical grains and an external film, Rosen gave a closed form for the outlet curve. Two dimensionless groups decide its shape.

    λ′ = 3D′KaH / (z w R²)    ψ = D′Ka / (R kc,g)    z = ε / (1 − ε)

    c/c₀ = ½ [1 + erf(E)]    E = (3τ′ / 2λ′ − 1) / (2 √((1 + 5ψ) / 5λ′))

  5. Numerical check

    Rosen ignores axial dispersion and only gives the outlet. The numerical model solves the column equations directly with a linear driving force for the grains and Edwards–Richardson dispersion, on 200 finite volumes. With kLDF = 15D′ / (R²(1 + 5ψ)) it has the same front width as Rosen when dispersion is switched off, and the test suite checks exactly that.

The stoichiometric time, where half the feed comes through, depends only on the mass balance and is identical in both modes. The corrections change how wide the front is, so they move the 5 % breakthrough time by roughly 10 to 15 %.

What the model leaves out

  • Only one refrigerant at a time. Mixtures need competitive isotherms (IAST or multicomponent Langmuir); the separation page overlays single curves as a first estimate.
  • A linear isotherm through one point at 0.8 bar. Near saturation the real isotherm is curved and the front self-sharpens.
  • Constant temperature. Heat of adsorption warms the bed and shortens the breakthrough in practice.
  • Knudsen diffusion in 4–9 Å pores. In pores this narrow transport is activated (configurational) diffusion, so D′ here is an upper bound.
  • Gas properties at 100 °C only, viscosities from CoolProp.
